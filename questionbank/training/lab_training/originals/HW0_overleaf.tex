\section*{Soldering}

Always wear safety glasses when soldering. Melted solder can ``pop,'' resulting in the ejection of a small drop of liquid metal. If the drop of liquid metal falls on your skin, you may feel a slight sting. If the drop of liquid metal falls into your eye, you will have a very bad day.

When you first power up the new soldering iron, please coat the tip with solder to prevent it from oxidizing right away. The soldering tip usually has a copper core with an iron surface. If this surface comes in contact with oxygen (air) at elevated temperatures, it will react to form iron oxide (``rust''). Iron oxides are thermal insulators that impede heat flow from the soldering tip.

Forming a protective layer of solder on the tip surface is called \emph{tinning the tip}. Please note that the soldering iron may take a couple of minutes to reach its operating temperature.
